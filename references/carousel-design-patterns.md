# Carousel Design Patterns

Extracted and generalized from proven LinkedIn carousels. Each pattern includes a slide map, draft syntax, theme compatibility, and reusable template.

---

## P1 — Narrative Story Arc

A story-driven carousel that builds tension through a question or problem, unfolds a narrative, and lands on a universal takeaway.

**Best for:** Thought leadership, personal branding, teaching through story

### Slide map

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook question** — a seemingly simple question that implies a deeper answer ("Copywriting or design — which comes first?") |
| 2 | **Setup / Old way** — describe the conventional method or belief |
| 3 | **Conflict** — why the old way falls short, or a historical turning point (Bill Bernbach, 1960) |
| 4 | **Resolution** — the reframe or new approach ("the idea comes first") |
| 5 | **Takeaway** — one-sentence philosophy + attribution/brand |
| 6 (opt) | **CTA** — soft redirect to newsletter or follow |

### Draft syntax
```md
## Copywriting or design?

"Copywriting or design?" he said, "which comes first?"

"Which comes first?" I said.

"Yeah, like, in general. When you're making an ad, does design follow copy or vice versa?"

|||

For a long time, ads were made a certain way.

The copywriter wrote the words, then slipped the work under the art director's door. The art director designed around the writer's concept and vision.

It wasn't collaborative. It was an assembly line.

|||

Until 1960. When creative director Bill Bernbach tried something new.

He put the copywriter and the art director in a room together. To think and work and create together. Simultaneously.

|||

Bernbach's "creative teams" went on to make remarkably innovative advertising — including the famous VW "Think Small" campaign.

Proof that the best ideas emerge when two people are thinking, not just one.

|||

So the idea comes first.

Not copy. Not design. The idea.

Marketing, first and foremost, is about ideas. And the best ideas come from collaboration from the get-go.
```

### Theme compatibility

| Theme | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| minimal | yes | Clean, lets the story breathe |
| dark | yes | Good for serious/contemplative tone |
| ink-yellow | yes | Warm, matches storytelling vibe |
| neon | maybe | Only if the story is edgy/disruptive |
| bold | maybe | Accent-heavy might distract from narrative |

### CTA patterns

- Soft: "More from [brand] — 100s of free resources at [link]"
- Mirror: "What's one idea that changed how you work?"
- Attribution: Brand logo or handle on the last slide

### Tips

- The first two slides carry 80% of the hook weight. Make the question feel universal but answerable.
- Word count markers ("219 words", "175 words left") create a progress signal — use sparingly.
- The "old way vs new way" contrast is the engine. Make the old way feel reasonable, not stupid.
- Use `[decorate]` for the title slide to get an emoji watermark in the theme accent.

## P2 — Educational Framework

A teaching carousel that identifies a specific audience problem, introduces a structured framework, and ends with a call to action.

**Best for:** Lead generation, list-building, establishing authority

### Slide map

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Audience hook** — "I help [specific audience]. They run successful businesses. But they struggle with [specific problem]." |
| 2 | **The problem** — what everyone gets wrong, in concrete terms |
| 3 | **The framework** — introduce 3 key questions or steps |
| 4 | **Deep dive** — explain each element with examples |
| 5 | **Application** — show framework in action on profile or content |
| 6 | **CTA** — "Comment KEYWORD to get the link" |

### Draft syntax

```md
## What everyone gets wrong about attracting clients on LinkedIn

My clients are consultants and agency owners. They run successful businesses. Outside of social media, they're doing fine.

But they struggle to attract clients on LinkedIn.

Because they fail to answer the fundamental question: What exactly do you do?

|||

Here's the problem. You know what you do. You've refined your offer. But your audience doesn't know.

You vaguely wave your hand in the direction of who you work with. You're not specific. You don't want to seem "too sales-y."

But people won't read between the lines.

|||

The fix is answering three key questions:

1 - Who do you work with?
2 - What will I get if I invest in working with you?
3 - Where is the proof that you can do what you say?

|||

We don't answer these once. We use every opportunity:

Profile Banner -> Who + Outcome + Social Proof
Headline -> Who + Outcome + Social Proof
About Section -> Who + Outcome
Experience Section -> Who + Outcome
Postscript -> Who + Outcome
Back page of carousels -> Who + Outcome

|||

[accent]Passive Lead Generation[/accent] covers 8 specific changes you can make right now to start attracting inbound leads with your profile and content.

Comment [accent]GUIDE[/accent] to get the link.
```

### Theme compatibility

| Theme | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| minimal | yes | Professional, readable |
| dark | yes | Authoritative feel |
| ink-yellow | yes | Warm, approachable |
| neon | no | Too distracting for educational content |
| bold | yes | Accent blue reinforces LinkedIn-native feel |

### CTA patterns

- Comment-gate: "Comment KEYWORD to get the link" (keyword should be one clear word)
- Link-in-bio: "Click the link in my profile to learn more"
- DM-open: "Send me a DM with KEYWORD and I'll share"

### Tips

- Name the audience explicitly in slide 1 — specificity signals authority.
- The three-question framework is the anchor. Number them for readability.
- Repeat the framework in grid format on slide 4 for visual variety.
- End with a specific, low-friction action. "Comment GUIDE" beats "Let me know what you think."
- Add `[decorate]` if you want emoji watermarks between slides.
- Use `[claude logo bottom]` for brand attribution on the final slide.

## P3 — Profile Anatomy Grid

A visual carousel that maps profile elements to messaging principles. Each slide dissects one component.

**Best for:** Profile optimization, personal branding, demonstrating expertise

### Slide map

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** — "Your profile is leaving money on the table." |
| 2 | **Banner** — Who + Outcome + Social Proof |
| 3 | **Headline** — Who + Outcome + Social Proof |
| 4 | **About section** — Who + Outcome |
| 5 | **Experience** — Who + Outcome + Social Proof |
| 6 | **Postscript / CTA** — reinforce everywhere |

### Draft syntax

```md
## Your profile anatomy cheat sheet

Every element of your profile sends a signal.

|||

[accent]Profile Banner[/accent]

Who + Outcome + Social Proof

Your banner is prime real estate. Don't waste it on a generic landscape.

|||

[accent]Headline[/accent]

Who + Outcome + Social Proof

This is the second thing people see after your photo.

|||

[accent]About Section[/accent]

Who + Outcome

Tell them who you help and what changes for them.

|||

[accent]Experience Section[/accent]

Who + Outcome + Social Proof

Results speak louder than responsibilities.

|||

[accent]Postscript / Carousel Back Page[/accent]

Who + Outcome

Every touchpoint is another chance to make it crystal clear.
```

### Theme compatibility

| Theme | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| minimal | yes | Clean grid layout |
| dark | yes | Good contrast for labels |
| ink-yellow | yes | Warm and approachable |
| neon | maybe | Works for bold section headers |
| bold | yes | Strong white-on-blue contrast |

### CTA patterns

- Principle-as-CTA: "Spell it out, over and over again."
- Soft: "Which section of your profile needs work?"
- Direct: "I help [audience] attract clients. Link in bio."

### Tips

- Use `[accent]` liberally for section labels — they pop against the theme accent color.
- One profile element per slide. Keep the formula consistent.
- The repeating "Who + Outcome + Social Proof" is the through-line — make it a refrain.
- P2 and P3 stack well together (framework first, then anatomy).

## P4 — Energetic / Algorithm Playbook

A high-energy carousel with bold voice, short punchy slides, and algorithm insights.

**Best for:** Personal brands, entertainer-educators, going viral

### Slide map

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Bold statement** — command or provocative phrase |
| 2 | **The algorithm truth** — counterintuitive insight |
| 3 | **Tactic 1** — what to do instead |
| 4 | **Tactic 2** — another punchy tip |
| 5 | **Wrap-up + CTA** |

### Draft syntax

```md
## EDIT WITH A FLAMING SWORD OF FIRE!

Always remember to think about what actually works.

Not what's complicated. What's simpler. Easier.

|||

Social media has a point where it feels impossible.

Trip back to what actually works:

Explore page, reels tab, search feed, stories, keywords in your captions, topics you post about consistently.

|||

The algorithm needs to know:

- What engages your audience
- What else they follow
- What they've recently engaged with
- What formats keep them visible

|||

Post content that's specific enough for the algorithm to know where to show it.

Interesting enough to appeal broadly.

Keep posting formats they've engaged with before.

|||

LINK IN BIO
```

### Theme compatibility

| Theme | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| minimal | no | Too subdued |
| dark | yes | Punchy, modern |
| ink-yellow | no | Too soft |
| neon | yes | Green accent matches the energy |
| bold | yes | White on blue = impact |

### CTA patterns

- Scarcity: "LINK IN BIO" (caps, no explanation)
- Challenge: "Try this for 7 days and report back"
- Engagement: "Which one are you trying this week?"

### Tips

- Keep slides to 1-3 lines max. The reader should feel speed.
- ALL CAPS for emphasis on key phrases (works well with `bold` or `neon`).
- No more than 5 slides. This pattern relies on density, not depth.
- Avoid `[accent]` — multiline rhythm matters more than highlights.
- The "counterintuitive insight" structure is the engine.
- Do NOT use `|||` auto-split — use manual markers for precise control.

## P5 — Case Study / Transformation

A results-driven carousel that tells a before-and-after story with specific metrics.

**Best for:** Agency owners, consultants, SaaS, social proof

### Slide map

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** — "How [client] did [result] in [timeframe]" |
| 2 | **Challenge** — the situation before, what wasn't working |
| 3 | **Solution** — what changed, what was implemented |
| 4 | **Results** — specific numbers (CPM, impressions, engagement) |
| 5 | **Key takeaway** — one principle that made the difference |
| 6 | **CTA** — "Want the same? Here's how." |

### Draft syntax

```md
## How Emma built a scalable marketing engine

Emma was handling marketing alone using manual processes for LinkedIn outreach and email.

Early results — yes. But scaling was difficult without infrastructure.

|||

[accent]Challenge[/accent]

Early success with marketing, but no infrastructure to scale. Losing the personal touch. Authenticity getting diluted.

They needed a system to scale without sacrificing the human connection.

|||

[accent]Solution[/accent]

When they found Zest, it empowered them to manage and scale outreach with streamlined discovery.

Centralized outreach replaced scattered email threads. Maintaining authentic relationships became simpler.

|||

[accent]Results[/accent]

100k+ impressions with a CPM of $22.36

Channels expanded: LinkedIn, YouTube

Data-driven hooks achieved 3.5x higher engagement than standard article-style posts.

|||

[accent]Key takeaway[/accent]

Focus on scaling while maintaining authentic relationships with your audience.

Technology should streamline operations — not replace the human touch.

|||

Want to build your own scalable system?

I help [audience] build marketing engines that grow.

Link in bio.
```

### Theme compatibility

| Theme | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| minimal | yes | Clean for metrics display |
| dark | yes | Numbers pop on dark bg |
| ink-yellow | yes | Warm for story-driven case study |
| neon | no | Flashy undermines credibility |
| bold | yes | Works for confident result claims |

### CTA patterns

- Soft lead: "Want the same? Here's how."
- Direct: "I help [audience] do this. Link in bio."
- Social proof CTA: "Got similar results? Share in the comments."

### Tips

- Lead with the metric in slide 1 if it's impressive. "100k impressions" hooks better than "Emma's story."
- Use `[accent]` for section labels (Challenge, Solution, Results, Takeaway) to create a visual rhythm.
- Keep the challenge relatable — the reader should think "that's me."
- End with one clear takeaway principle, not a list. Leave them with one thing to remember.
- Metrics with dollar signs ($22.36 CPM) and high numbers (3.5x) are screenshot-bait.

## Pattern selection guide

| Goal | Best pattern | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Establish thought leadership | P1 Narrative | Story earns authority without selling |
| Generate leads | P2 Educational Framework | Problem -> framework -> CTA is proven |
| Profile optimization content | P3 Anatomy Grid | Tactical, saves easily |
| Go viral / stand out | P4 Energetic | Pattern-interrupt format |
| Close clients / prove value | P5 Case Study | Social proof with hard numbers |
| Repurpose a newsletter issue | P1 or P5 | Narrative or transformation both work |
| Launch a lead magnet | P2 + P3 stack | Framework + profile tactics = high value |

## Theme-pattern pairing

| Pattern | Recommended theme | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Narrative | ink-yellow or minimal | Warmth or neutrality serves the story |
| P2 Educational | bold or minimal | Authority without distraction |
| P3 Anatomy | bold or ink-yellow | Labels need contrast |
| P4 Energetic | neon or dark | Energy needs visual backing |
| P5 Case Study | minimal or dark | Numbers need clean display |

## Engine support notes

All 5 patterns are fully supported by `lib/carousel.py`:

- `|||` manual markers for slide splitting — required for P1, P4; optional for P2, P3, P5
- `[accent]` spans — supported for P2, P3, P5 section labels
- `[decorate]` — supported for P1 emoji watermarks
- `[claude logo]` / `[claude logo bottom]` — supported for brand attribution on any pattern
- All 5 themes (minimal, dark, ink-yellow, neon, bold) — available via `theme=` parameter
- Preview mode: `--preview` or `preview_only=True` for testing before PDF generation
